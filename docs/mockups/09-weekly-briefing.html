One Phone Asked For A Spyware Ad Server. Nothing Else Did.

On Tuesday evening the kitchen Pixel looked up a host from the AdFection indicator set three times in seven seconds. Your Pi-hole lists did not cover it, so the lookups were answered. A DNS lookup is not an infection, but it means an ad on that phone reached the delivery network.

The domain is now blocked, along with 26 other indicator domains your lists were missing. The phone has no agent, so TheySpy cannot see further; the next step is a Mobile Verification Toolkit check with the STIX bundle from the Evidence page.

Patch Two Browsers And One Phone This Week

Two flaws that are being exploited in the wild touch your devices. Both have fixes already:

  • Chrome V8 type confusion (CVE-2026-4127): on the MacBook Pro, the work laptop and the Pixel. Restart Chrome after it updates. CISA wants federal systems fixed by 17 November.
  • WebKit use-after-free (CVE-2026-4092): iPhone 16 and iPad, fixed in iOS 19.0.3.
  • NAS firmware (CVE-2026-3810): the Synology is only reachable on the LAN, which lowers the risk, but update DSM.

Your Internet Address Is A Target Signal

Reuters reported this week that an ad-tech firm alleged to work with spyware vendors was the top bidder on Starlink users’ ad traffic in more than a hundred countries. Your public address belongs to Starlink. Gateway blocking already strips most ad traffic; routing browsing through a VPN exit removes the satellite signature for what gets through.
